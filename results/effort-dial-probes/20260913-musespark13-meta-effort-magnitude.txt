Does Meta's effort dial change how much Muse Spark 1.3 THINKS? - 2026-09-13

VERDICT: YES AT THE BOTTOM, NO AT THE TOP. A FOUR-RUNG DIAL SOLD AS SIX. minimal, low and medium
occupy bands that do not touch each other or anything above them. high, xhigh and max overlap almost
entirely and cannot be told apart at this sample size. So the field is not merely READ on this
endpoint - which the sibling receipt already established by two-layer validation and an echoed tier -
it CHANGES THE WORK, across the bottom half of its range and not the top.

    POST https://api.meta.ai/v1/responses, model muse-spark-1.3, reasoning:{effort:...}
    One fixed prompt (a concurrency-reasoning question with an exhaustive-enumeration instruction),

    minimal     668,  823,   962        817     2377- 2823        17-22s
    low        1760, 2254,  2701       2238     3454- 4367        24-30s
    medium     5394, 5691,  6237       5774     6849- 7994        50-56s

    Every call echoed back the tier it was asked for. No 429s, no retries, no fallbacks.

THE MAGNITUDE READING, BY THIS BOARD'S RULE: no gap counts until it clears the worst within-condition
spread. The worst within-tier spread here is max, 17383/7928 = 2.19x. The bottom-to-top ratio of
means is 12655/817 = 15.5x, which clears it by seven times over. So the dial is real.

WHERE IT BINDS, PRECISELY. Four disjoint bands, not six:

    minimal  [  668,   962 ]  ---- disjoint
    low      [ 1760,  2701 ]  ---- disjoint
    medium   [ 5394,  6237 ]  ---- disjoint
    high     [ 7824, 11668 ]  \
    xhigh    [ 8348, 14335 ]   >-- mutually overlapping, indistinguishable at n=3
    max      [ 7928, 17383 ]  /

medium's ceiling (6237) sits below high's floor (7824), so the separation survives all the way up to
high. Above that it stops: max's range CONTAINS most of high's, and a single max call (7928) thought
less than two of the three high calls. The means still rise - 9385, 10757, 12655 - and the ranges say
that ordering is not resolvable here. Establishing it would need a much larger n, and the honest
statement at n=3 is that paying for max over high buys nothing measurable.

THIS IS THE SAME SHAPE ALIBABA'S DIAL SHOWS, ON A DIFFERENT VENDOR'S STACK. Both Qwen3.8 probes on
this board found a dial that binds hard at the bottom and saturates across the top three tiers, with
means that do not order cleanly. Two vendors, two serving stacks, same shape. That is worth noticing
precisely because it is the shape a buyer is least likely to assume: the expensive end of a published
effort dial is where it stops doing anything.

WHAT THIS CHANGES FOR THE BOARD. The Muse Spark 1.3 first-party rows can carry `verified` rather than
`first_party`, because the levels are now separated on this serving path rather than merely accepted
by it. The max row carries `verified_ceiling`: max is the top of the dial that exists - `none` is
refused CLI-side before any call, `ultra` is a closed feature gate that silently substitutes xhigh
(receipt: 20260912-musecode-cli-effort-vs-wire.txt), and nothing above max is servable - and the
ceiling is REAL BUT FLAT, in the same sense the Qwen rows are.

AND IT MAKES A PREDICTION THE NEXT ARM WILL TEST. If high and max are indistinguishable in thinking
volume, a bench leg at high should land near the max row's 33/105, and the 18/105 the same model
scored through OpenRouter should then be attributable to the ROUTE rather than to the tier. That leg
is running. This receipt is written before its result is known, which is the only order in which a
prediction is worth anything.

WHAT THIS RECEIPT DOES NOT SHOW. Thinking volume is not quality. Nothing here says the extra tokens
at max are useful, or that minimal's 817 are insufficient - only that the tiers command different
amounts of work. It is also one prompt: a dial that separates on a concurrency-reasoning question
might behave differently on a refactor or a search task. n=3 per tier supports the bottom-half
separation comfortably and supports no claim at all about the ordering of the top three.


================================================================================================
OUTCOME - APPENDED 2026-09-13, AFTER BOTH LEGS RAN. THE PREDICTION ABOVE IS FALSIFIED.
================================================================================================

The prediction above is left exactly as published. It said that if high and max are indistinguishable
in thinking volume, a bench leg at high should land near the max row's 33/105. Both rungs below max
have now been run on the real benchmark:

    tier   bench score   reasoning tokens (bench)   list cost   wall
    high      19/105            178,300              $10.81     64.5 min
    xhigh     20/105            225,766              $15.58     73.0 min
    max       33/105            286,242              $19.83    102.3 min

high landed at 19, not near 33. The top of this dial is TWO rungs, not one and not three: high and
xhigh are within a point of each other, and max is thirteen points above both.

WHICH HALF OF THIS RECEIPT SURVIVED. The GROUPING did. high and xhigh really are one rung - the toy
prompt could not separate them, and neither can the benchmark, on score or on tokens. The error was
extending that group upward to max, which is the half a buyer pays for.

WHAT THE TOKEN COLUMN DOES ON THE REAL WORKLOAD, WHICH IS THE PART WORTH KEEPING. Reasoning volume
rises by almost exactly 27% at each of the top two rungs - 178,300 to 225,766 to 286,242 - and the
score does not move for the first 27% and jumps thirteen on the second. So the failure here is NOT
that this receipt mis-measured the tokens. It measured them correctly and the step sizes on the bench
are the same shape. The failure is that token volume is not a proxy for capability gain at all: equal
increments of thinking bought nothing and then bought everything.

THE RULE THIS BOARD TAKES FROM IT. A magnitude probe shows whether a dial is CONNECTED. It does not
show what a rung is WORTH, and it cannot be used to argue that one tier is not worth paying for. Only
a bench pair can do that. Applied backwards, the same caution attaches to the Qwen3.8 saturation
findings cited above: those are statements about thinking volume on a fixed prompt, and no bench pair
was ever run to test whether Alibaba's top rungs differ in score.

Per-repo detail, because the aggregate hides a sign flip: repo 1 at xhigh spent 178,161 reasoning
tokens against high's 115,670 (up 54%), while repo 2 at xhigh spent 47,605 against high's 62,630
(down 24%). The combined +27% is one repo carrying it. xhigh is not separated from high by anything.
